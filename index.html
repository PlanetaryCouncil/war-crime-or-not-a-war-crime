War Crime or Not

Content warning. Text descriptions of killings of children, medics and journalists, and of sexual violence. No images.

Two cases. Which is worse?

You will see pairs of incidents from Gaza since October 2023. For each pair, pick the one you judge more serious. Thousands of these small choices add up to a ranking, the same way chess ratings (Elo) come from individual games.

Every incident lists its sources and how solid the evidence is (documented reported alleged), the official Israeli response where one exists, and what governments said.

Before and after the comparisons you will also be asked to rate a few BBC headlines. That part is an experiment, explained on the Method page once you finish, so it doesn't influence your answers.

    Method

    Ranking

    Each vote is one “game” between two incidents. Ratings are recalculated from the full vote log and averaged over 20 random orderings, so early and late voters count equally. Pairs are chosen to fill gaps: incidents with fewer votes, and close match-ups, come up more often.

    The ranking measures how serious participants judge each incident to be. That is not a legal finding. Whether an act is a war crime is decided by courts applying the laws of war (distinction, proportionality, precaution, and specific offences like attacking medical units or using starvation as a weapon).

    The headline experiment

    Everyone rates the same set of BBC headlines, but a random half of the headlines is shown before the comparisons and the other half after. If reading documented detail changes how a headline looks, the before and after averages for that headline will differ.

    What it can show: whether exposure to the incident record shifts judgements of the headline. What it can't show on its own: whether the shifted judgement is the correct one. The comparisons show the gravest material, so some shift is expected from priming alone. Read the result as “how the headline looks next to the record”, not as a verdict.

    Headlines were picked because someone publicly criticised their framing, from either direction. Each one links to that criticism.

    Evidence labels

    • documented: video, forensic or multiple independent investigations, or acknowledged by the Israeli military.
    • reported: credible outlets or NGOs, not independently confirmed.
    • alleged: rests mainly on testimony.

    Condemn-o-meter

    Scores the wording of official reactions from 1 (“concerned”) to 4 (“categorically condemns”). It measures words only, not what the government then did.
